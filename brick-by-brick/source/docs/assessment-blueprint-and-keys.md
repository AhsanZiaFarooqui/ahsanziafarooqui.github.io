# Fixed assessment blueprint and answer key

Version: comparison-v2. Two topics; two forms per topic; eight items per form; three choices plus “I’m not sure”. Every correct response scores one; every incorrect or uncertain response scores zero. Missing items are not responses. A score is missing until all eight items are submitted.

Forms A and B are designed to match concepts, formats, approximate difficulty and reading demand. They are **not psychometrically validated**. Pilot them, review item-level performance and form-order effects, and freeze a version before a real trial. Do not revise items midway without documenting a new version and the affected sessions.

Each form samples two items from each row:

| Topic | Concept | Item positions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| time | Hands and o’clock | 1, 2 |
| time | Counting minutes | 3, 4 |
| time | Half past and quarter hours | 5, 6 |
| time | Combining hours and minutes | 7, 8 |
| fractions | Whole and equal parts | 1, 2 |
| fractions | Numerator, denominator and pictures | 3, 4 |
| fractions | Equivalent fractions | 5, 6 |
| fractions | Comparing equal wholes | 7, 8 |

Independent fair random draws assign A→B or B→A at session creation and standard or AI at completion of the initial quiz. Both arms receive the same fixed final form for a given initial form. The draws are 1:1 probabilities, not forced equal totals in small samples.

Teaching uses separate worked-* and guided-* pools in curriculum.ts. No scored clock time, scored fraction picture, equivalence target, or comparison pair is used as a tutorial example. Conceptual overlap is intentional: the same learning objectives must be taught. Basic hand roles and definitions necessarily recur. Guided practice uses teaching values and explanations, never scored item IDs. AI chooses only checked content from these pools.

Descriptions below specify the visual, including non-answer-revealing clock positions for screen readers. Visual labels and instructional notes are removed during scored questions. No correctness feedback, hints, or answer keys appear until the final quiz is complete.

## time — Form A

### time-A-1: Hands and o’clock

Which hand tells the hours?

Visual description: A clock has a short thick orange hand and a long thin blue hand.

1. The short, thick hand
2. The long, thin hand
3. Both hands do the same job
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 1. The short, thick hand**

Hours use the short hand. The hands have different jobs.

### time-A-2: Hands and o’clock

Read this clock.

Visual description: Short hand at 8; long hand at 12.

1. 12:40
2. 8:00
3. 8:12
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 2. 8:00**

The long hand at twelve means zero minutes past. Read the short hand for the hour.

### time-A-3: Counting minutes

How many minutes past does the long hand show?

Visual description: Long hand at 4.

1. 4
2. 40
3. 20
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. 20**

4 groups of five make 20 minutes.

### time-A-4: Counting minutes

How many minutes past does the long hand show?

Visual description: Long hand at 10.

1. 50
2. 10
3. 10 minutes to
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 1. 50**

10 groups of five make 50 minutes past. The question asks for minutes past.

### time-A-5: Half past and quarter hours

Which phrase matches this clock?

Visual description: Long hand at 6; short hand halfway from 4 to 5.

1. Half past five
2. Half past four
3. Quarter past four
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 2. Half past four**

Half past is thirty minutes after the hour that has started.

### time-A-6: Half past and quarter hours

Which phrase matches this clock?

Visual description: Long hand at 9; short hand between 8 and 9, closer to 9.

1. Quarter past eight
2. Quarter to eight
3. Quarter to nine
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. Quarter to nine**

Forty-five minutes have passed. Fifteen remain until the next hour: quarter to the next hour.

### time-A-7: Combining hours and minutes

Read the hour and minutes together.

Visual description: Short hand just after 5; long hand at 2.

1. 5:10
2. 6:10
3. 5:02
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 1. 5:10**

Read the hour the short hand has passed. Count five minutes for each number reached by the long hand.

### time-A-8: Combining hours and minutes

Read the hour and minutes together.

Visual description: Short hand between 11 and 12; long hand at 8.

1. 12:40
2. 11:08
3. 11:40
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. 11:40**

The short hand moves continuously. The approaching hour has not started yet.

## fractions — Form A

### fractions-A-1: Whole and equal parts

Which pieces belong to the whole strip?

Visual description: One outlined strip with five equal parts; 2 shaded.

1. Only the shaded pieces
2. All five pieces
3. Only the unshaded pieces
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 2. All five pieces**

The whole includes both shaded and unshaded pieces inside the border.

### fractions-A-2: Whole and equal parts

A strip is cut into five pieces. One piece is much larger than the others. Are these equal parts?

Visual description: No picture is needed.

1. Yes, because they came from one strip
2. Yes, because we can count them
3. No, the pieces must be the same size
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. No, the pieces must be the same size**

Equal parts of one whole must have equal size, not just belong to the same strip.

### fractions-A-3: Numerator, denominator and pictures

In 3/7, what does the bottom number 7 tell us?

Visual description: Read the fraction in the question.

1. Seven equal parts make the whole
2. Seven parts are selected
3. There are seven separate wholes
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 1. Seven equal parts make the whole**

The denominator counts all equal parts in one whole. The numerator counts selected parts.

### fractions-A-4: Numerator, denominator and pictures

What fraction of the whole is shaded?

Visual description: One whole with five equal large parts; 3 shaded.

1. 5/3
2. 2/5
3. 3/5
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. 3/5**

Count all five equal parts below the line and the selected parts above it.

### fractions-A-5: Equivalent fractions

Which fraction equals 2/6?

Visual description: Use the same whole.

1. 1/6
2. 1/3
3. 2/3
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 2. 1/3**

Divide both numerator and denominator by two. The amount stays the same.

### fractions-A-6: Equivalent fractions

Which fraction equals 3/9?

Visual description: Use the same whole.

1. 1/3
2. 3/3
3. 1/9
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 1. 1/3**

Group each three ninths into a third. Divide both counts by three.

### fractions-A-7: Comparing equal wholes

For the same-sized whole, which is larger: 1/5 or 1/10?

Visual description: Both fractions refer to identical whole sizes.

1. 1/10
2. They are equal
3. 1/5
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 3. 1/5**

Dividing the same whole into more equal pieces makes each piece smaller.

### fractions-A-8: Comparing equal wholes

These wholes are identical. Which shaded amount is larger?

Visual description: Two equal-sized wholes divided into seven equal parts each. Top: 2 selected; bottom: 5 selected.

1. The top amount
2. The bottom amount
3. They are equal
4. I’m not sure.

**Answer: 2. The bottom amount**

With the same whole and denominator, more selected parts means a larger fraction.
