# 3–5-minute ILAP showcase outline

Use this as a structure, not a script that invents student experiences. Replace blank evidence only with real observations. Label every demonstration learner as simulated unless a participant actually consented to the use of their anonymous results. Do not mix demo sessions into trial evidence; export or remove demo-only visits before real trials, or use a separate browser profile.

**0:00–0:35 — A problem I investigated.** The student states a real difficulty she personally identified: “I noticed __________. My evidence was __________.” If this is not yet known, say the proposed problem is being investigated. Explain the idea that different confusions in the same maths lesson may need different explanations. State Digital Transformation and SDG 4: Quality Education as intended links.

**0:35–1:20 — Two simulated learners, two paths.** Start separate Time checks. For simulated learner A, answer the first three and fourth questions correctly. For simulated learner B, select 6:15 on 3:30, the shorter hand for minutes, and an incorrect quarter phrase; then answer the focused fourth item incorrectly. On the fifth question, show the differing follow-ups. Grown-ups records the actual selected IDs/reasons. The adaptive model is a prototype, not a statement about either child's fixed ability. Use `scripts/demo-paths.ts` for the current reproducible paths if the bank changes.

**1:20–2:00 — A clock lesson.** Show a targeted lesson selected after the simulated check. For hand confusion use Meet the two hands; for continuous hour movement show The hour hand keeps moving. Demonstrate Next step/Pause: at 3:30 the hour hand is halfway to four, at 3:45 three-quarters of the way. The same trusted time state drives both hands. A grown-up may need to arrange the specific simulated responses ahead of the talk; do not claim it is a live child's diagnosis.

**2:00–2:45 — A fractions lesson.** In a separate simulated Fractions visit, show a wrong symbol/quantity answer and its distinguishing probe. Use the selected fractions lesson, or demonstrate equivalence through a targeted F4 path. Highlight that subdivision keeps the shaded amount fixed and the whole lengths identical. Different symbols can represent the same amount. The app includes both halves/quarters and thirds/sixths lessons.

**2:45–3:15 — Meaningful AI, accurately labelled.** If enabled and actually verified, choose Explain differently and enter a short anonymous reasoning explanation. Show the Live AI wording label and a checked visual. Explain that AI rewords the maths but cannot score answers or alter the probability model. If no live connection is enabled, clearly demonstrate **built-in explanation mode** and say that the live function awaits credentials/verification. Do not describe code-generation help as the app's live educational AI role.

**3:15–3:45 — Try, then check.** Show one guided answer with a hint and a fresh independent question without hints. Explain that the app keeps these different kinds of evidence separate and does not mark a skill learned just because an animation played. Point to the optional later revisit.

**3:45–4:30 — Real feedback and changes.** Once collected, show one genuine anonymous observation, the student's design decision, the revision and what happened on retesting. Otherwise say these activities remain to be done. Avoid causal learning-gain claims from a small before–after trial. Synthetic model-audit results must be labelled software simulations, not learners' progress.

**4:30–5:00 — Ownership and continuation.** The student explains a decision she understands, acknowledges specific adult/AI/coding assistance, and names the next small improvement and maintenance plan. No promise of eligibility or selection.

Rehearse with the actual device and check the short version fits three minutes by reducing the feedback/technical explanation, not by removing honest labels.
