AdaptiveChoice Lab

A synthetic adaptive-choice experiment that learns one respondent's willingness to pay for automated rather than human assessment.

Methodological disclosure: This is a one-parameter demonstration with a discrete uniform prior and a binary logistic choice model. It is not the multi-parameter BACE design used in the author's dissertation and makes no claim about real taxpayers.

Choices made

    Stress test: adaptive versus fixed questions

    Simulate 60 synthetic respondents, six binary choices each, using paired seeded response draws and the same likelihood used for Bayesian updating. Compare mean absolute error (MAE) in estimated latent preference.

    This is an in-model simulation, not out-of-sample validation. Differences are scenario-dependent and do not prove adaptive designs universally outperform fixed designs.

    Model specification and limitations

    Latent θ lies on a discrete grid from −7,500 to +7,500 PKR, with equal prior weights. Conditional probability of choosing automated assessment given an extra bill cost c is logistic((θ − c)/1,800). Bayes' rule updates posterior grid weights. The next c maximizes expected entropy reduction among remaining candidate costs. The model holds all non-price attributes constant and ignores response fatigue, framing, errors in cost perception, and population-level estimation.
